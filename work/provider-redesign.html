Redesigned a £9B wealth management platform for UHNW clients

As the founding designer, I shaped the initial design of a back-office platform supporting £9B in client assets. This case study highlights the redesign of portfolio management for providers and tax wrappers, improving data accessibility and advisor workflows.

01

Overview

Centralise client data, improve navigation, and enhance data accessibility for teams including advisers, analysts, and data specialists.

Background

At the time, client data was fragmented across the back-office system and Salesforce, with no centralised location to access key information. This created friction for advisers and analysts who needed to rely on complete and accurate data during client interactions.

Key problems

Inefficient workflowsSwitching between platforms wastes time and disrupts conversations.
Incomplete dataMissing information made it harder to provide high-quality advice.
UX debtsCluttered and unintuitive interface reduces confidence in daily use.

Solution

Unified platformCentralise key tools and data to reduce context switching.
Complete data accessEnsure all essential client data is accurate and up-to-date.
Improved UIRedesign interface to reduce friction and training time.

Challenges

Balancing user needsDesigned for advisers, analysts, and operations staff with distinct workflows.
Handling heavy dataSimplified access to large volumes of complex financial information.
Simplifying interfaceReduced friction and training time by making the UI more intuitive.
Parallel ProjectsBalanced design work while supporting three other ongoing projects.

Outcomes

48% faster data accessRetrieval time dropped after centralising client data.
36% fewer support ticketsInternal tickets related to data access decreased.
29% higher user satisfactionUsers reported improved confidence and ease of use.

Incomplete data

Senior pension adviser

03

Ideation

Identify and audit missing or incomplete data in the Back Office

  • Interview advisers and analysts to identify the key data they rely on for quality advice.
  • Map required data vs. current back office data to highlight missing pieces.
  • Ask them to rate data importance to help us prioritise what to add now vs. later.

Prioritise and categorise the data based on both data team and adviser needs, as well as its impact on the advice process.

  • Collaborate with advisers and the data team to rank and group data by importance and usability.
  • Focus on data that directly influences advice quality, ensuring alignment between technical feasibility and business value.
04

Design & Iteration

Translate data requirements into low-fidelity wireframes and test rapidly

  • Translated prioritised data into low-fidelity wireframes.
  • Proposed new navigation supporting multiple accounts and providers.
  • Rapid feedback loops validated structure and clarity.
05

User Feedback

Validate designs through feedback

  • Does the data grouping make sense?
  • Is anything missing?
  • Is the navigation clear and intuitive?

Key insight 1

Different tax wrappers require distinct datasets

With variations even within ISAs that need to be accounted for.

Key insight 2

Dialog-style navigation is not optimal

As users often need to switch between multiple accounts under different providers.

Key insight 3

Most portfolios contain 1–2 accounts

But in edge cases may include up to 10. Design must optimise space efficiency across both scenarios.

09

Retrospective

What went well

Unified workflows across user types, streamlined data access, and built a scalable foundation for future enhancements.

  • Successfully unified workflows for multiple user types.
  • Improved data visibility and navigation, reducing friction and support tickets.
  • Delivered a scalable foundation for future features.

Lessons learned

Early alignment with the data team, rapid user testing, and prioritised trade-offs ensured efficient validation and an MVP-focused delivery.

  • Early alignment with the data and advice team is critical when handling heavy and complex datasets.
  • Iterative testing with real users accelerates validation and reduces rework.
  • Trade-offs (like postponing the data quality level) are necessary for MVP focus.

Impact

Faster access to accurate client data, reduced training time, and a foundation for future UHNW management enhancements.

  • Advisers and analysts can now access accurate client data faster.
  • Reduced training time and increased confidence in system use.
  • Created a design foundation for further enhancements to support UHNW client management.
